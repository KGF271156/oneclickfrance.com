HAUTE CUISINE AND DAILY BREAD

Gastronomy, region by region.

French food is recognised by UNESCO as part of the world’s intangible heritage. From the daily baguette to the great cheeses and wines, here is where it all comes from.

The everyday

Bread, the boulangerie and the table

The daily baguette

Many French people buy their bread fresh once or twice a day. The “baguette de tradition” is made with flour, water, salt and yeast only, and is protected by law from additives.

The boulangerie and the pâtisserie

Croissants and pains au chocolat at breakfast, tarts and éclairs on Sunday: the local bakery is the heart of the village or neighbourhood.

The meal as a ritual

Starter, main course, cheese and dessert, with bread on the table and time to talk. Lunch can last two hours, and the meal itself is on UNESCO’s list.

Haute cuisine

Escoffier, Bocuse and the Michelin guide: France’s great chefs have made French cooking the model of fine dining.

The cheeseboard

More than 1,000 cheeses

Comté

A hard cow’s-milk cheese from the Jura mountains in Burgundy-Franche-Comté, aged for up to 24 months.

Brie de Meaux

A soft, creamy cheese with a white rind from Île-de-France, called the “king of cheeses”.

Camembert de Normandie

The best-known Norman cheese, soft and rich, with a bloomy rind.

Roquefort

A blue sheep’s-milk cheese ripened in the caves of Roquefort-sur-Soulzon in Occitanie.

Reblochon and Munster

Washed-rind cheeses from the Alps and from Alsace, essential for tartiflette and for a hearty cheeseboard.

Chèvre from the Loire

Crottin de Chavignol, Sainte-Maure and Valençay are goat’s cheeses that go perfectly with Sancerre.

In the glass

Regional wines

Bordeaux

Blends of Cabernet and Merlot from the Médoc, Saint-Émilion and Pomerol; sweet Sauternes. Nouvelle-Aquitaine.

Burgundy

Pinot Noir and Chardonnay from the Côte d’Or and Chablis: small plots, big names. Bourgogne-Franche-Comté.

Champagne

The sparkling wine of Reims and Épernay, made only in the Champagne region. Grand Est.

Alsace

Riesling, Gewurztraminer and Pinot Gris along the wine road. Grand Est.

The Loire Valley

Sancerre, Vouvray, Chinon and Muscadet, from crisp whites to light reds.

The Rhône, Provence and Languedoc

Châteauneuf-du-Pape, Côtes du Rhône, Provence rosé and the big, sunny reds of the Midi.
