THE FRENCH WAY OF LIFE

Savoir-vivre, the art of living.

What makes France stand out, and what the French abroad miss most.

What makes France stand out

Four things visitors notice

Gastronomy and daily bread

Food is a cultural pillar, from the boulangerie on the corner to the Sunday lunch. The meal is meant to be shared and savoured, not rushed.

Work-life balance

The French work to live, not live to work: long lunches, five weeks of paid holiday, and a strong sense that evenings, weekends and August belong to family and friends.

Landscape and geography

Alps and Pyrenees, Atlantic and Mediterranean coasts, vineyards, lavender fields and Paris: few countries pack so much variety into a day’s drive.

The art of politeness

Saying “bonjour” when you enter a shop, “merci, bonne journée” when you leave, and “vous” with people you do not know: small rituals that make everyday life smoother.

What the French abroad miss most

The small things that are hard to export

Bread and cheese culture

A proper baguette, a cheese course after the main dish, and a market stall that sells ten kinds of goat’s cheese.

Café terrace culture

A coffee that can last an hour, a table on the pavement and no one hurrying you to leave.

The marché routine

Twice-weekly open-air markets where you know the stallholders, choose your produce by sight and smell, and stop to chat.

Walkable and connected life

Villages and cities where you can walk to the bakery, the pharmacy and the school, and catch a fast train to the next city.

Casual social formality

The balance between tu and vous, the bise on the cheek, and a confidence that good manners and good humour go together.
